1. Brand & Positioning

The first few seconds should make it obvious who the product is, who it is for and what level of quality it represents.
Priority: High
Primary issue
“BetterSkin.app” versus “Better Solutions” creates immediate brand confusion.

What’s not working

The visitor cannot immediately tell whether BetterSkin is the company, the product, or simply the URL. “Better Solutions” reads like a different business.

Why it matters

Brand ambiguity reduces trust at the exact moment the visitor is deciding whether this is established, credible and worth exploring.

Recommendation

Create one clear hierarchy: brand → product → descriptor. If “Better Solutions” is the company, explain that relationship once and clearly.

Positioning opportunity

The product feels more premium than the current $12–$19 framing. Compare its value against the combined cost and effort of multiple apps and services.

2. Core Beliefs & Messaging

The strongest copy on the page is not the feature copy it is the belief system underneath it.
Priority: Critical
Your body changes as one system. Your apps don’t.

Patterns you’d never spot on your own.

Sleep, cycle, energy and skin are rarely separate stories.

One pattern can explain a whole bad week.

Why not the apps you already have?

Stay ahead of your body, not a step behind it.

Recommendation

Move these beliefs much higher on the page. They are the ideas a visitor can emotionally and intellectually agree with before they understand the details of the product.

3. Value Proposition & Offer

The outcome is strong. The language should make the prize clearer than the effort required to get there.
Priority: High

Core value

Bring wearable data, cycle data, hormone panels, blood tests, supplements, symptoms and skin into one connected view.

Better promise

“One weekly report instead of six apps” is stronger than “one dashboard instead of six dashboards.” The user cares about the usable result, not the interface count.

Sell the prize

Do not repeatedly sell “three minutes.” Sell the fact that a small setup can save substantial time, reduce mental load and surface useful insight in seconds.

Premium framing

Show what people would otherwise spend in time, app fees or fragmented appointments to get the same picture manually.

4. Copy & Content Clarity

Several phrases feel internally logical but are not immediately clear to a first-time visitor.
Priority: High

“Your whole body, one view”

Too abstract. “Whole body” and “one view” both require interpretation. Consider clearer wording around connected health information in one place.

“Take the 3-minute check”

The user does not know what is being checked. Better: “Take the 3-minute symptom check” or “Get started in three minutes.”

“No daily logging”

Ambiguous. Better to say: “We sync with your devices so you don’t have to manually log every day.”

“Visits that count”

Less ownable and less clear than “doctor-ready.” Use “Doctor-ready” as the lead concept, then explain what that means.

“Create PDF”

Use a familiar PDF/export icon rather than extra instructional text when the action is obvious visually.

“Join the founding list”

Interesting but unclear. If this is a feedback cohort, say what the user is joining and why it benefits them.

5. Page Hierarchy & User Flow

The page currently asks for action and explains features before the visitor has enough context to care.
Priority: Critical
Recommended journey
  1. Start with the belief: the body changes as one system, but current tools fragment the picture.
  2. Visually show the fragmentation different apps and body systems disconnected.
  3. Show the BetterSkin promise: one connected view/report.
  4. Demonstrate the patterns the user would not spot alone.
  5. Preview the weekly report and explain the experience.
  6. Then introduce trust markers, doctor-ready outputs, product depth, pricing and CTA.

Progressive disclosure

Move technical detail, secondary features and edge cases into expandable sections, secondary pages or the FAQ. The homepage should build desire and understanding, not explain the entire product.

6. UI & Visual Design

The product category calls for calm, clarity and emotional reassurance. The current page is sometimes too dense and literal.
Priority: Medium–High

More breathing room

Increase spacing and padding. Let each message land before introducing the next panel, button or explanatory block.

Reduce text over imagery

The current hero overlay makes the call to action harder to read. Use cleaner contrast or move text off photography.

Make pills ownable

The pill treatment appears repeatedly. Turn it into a recognisable brand ui using consistent shapes, spacing, iconography or emoji-like character.

Use friendlier iconography

“Private by design,” “validated symptom scale” and “doctor-ready” are strong trust points but currently feel buried. Give them clear icons and visual status. Maybe even a large section so they stand out more..

Visualise connection

Instead of writing that every app sees one part of the user, show disconnected body systems or app fragments merging into one connected picture.

Keep the product feeling premium

Calm whitespace, strong hierarchy, high-quality product previews and restraint will better support the value of the offer.

7. UX & Interaction Design

The site can teach through interaction instead of forcing the user to read everything.
Priority: High

Clickable insight cards

The sleep, cycle, hormone or skin panels could flip or expand on tap to reveal a real example report. This invites exploration and demonstrates the product.

Give the weekly report its own moment

The report is one of the strongest value propositions. Show exactly what it looks like and what it feels like to receive.

Encourage interaction

Clickable modules create engagement and let people discover details at their own pace instead of scrolling through dense explanations. This is trackable with analytics

Navigation polish

The menu currently jumps to the footer. A product that promises clarity and organisation should also communicate that through clean, reliable site navigation.

8. Product Experience & Feature Communication

Clarify what the user actually experiences day to day, weekly and when preparing for appointments.
Priority: High

Weekly versus daily

“Weekly report” is compelling, but make it clear whether users can also see current or daily insights. Avoid creating the impression that they must wait a week for useful information.

Device syncing

Frame syncing as effortless automation: the app does the compiling behind the scenes so the user does not have to.

Medication / dosage tracking

The “coming soon” dosage area is potentially valuable but currently appears abruptly. Give it a clear heading and a simple visual before asking users to interpret technical details.

Doctor-ready output

This is a powerful, ownable product concept. Treat it as a major benefit rather than a small supporting label.

9. Conversion, Trust & Value Equation

The dream outcome is clear. The biggest missing element is perceived likelihood of achievement.
Priority: Critical
Hormozi value equation lens

The page communicates the dream outcome and is beginning to reduce effort/sacrifice. What is underdeveloped is proof that users are likely to achieve the promised outcome, and how quickly value appears.

Social proof

Add testimonials, user numbers, beta feedback, clinician feedback, early outcomes or meaningful quotes.

External credibility

If there has been press, publication coverage, partnerships or expert validation, surface it clearly.

Time to value

Create a behavioural promise such as: connect your data tonight, wake up with a clearer picture tomorrow. Make the first useful result feel close.

Effort reduction

Quantify the work the product removes. “What normally takes two hours across six apps can be understood in 30 seconds” is more compelling than repeatedly saying the setup takes three minutes.

Pricing

$12–$19 currently feels inexpensive relative to the breadth of the proposition. Test whether premium pricing better signals value and sustainability.

FAQ as a conversion tool

Move cluttering explanatory content into a richer FAQ. Twenty useful questions may be more effective than squeezing every objection into the core page.

10. Story, Emotion & Human Connection

The page needs more heart not more explanation.
Priority: Critical
What is missing
Why does this matter to you?

The visitor should understand the human reason this product exists, not only the technical reason it works.

Founder / origin story

Add a concise human story explaining what experience or frustration led to the product being created.

Sell the feeling

The audience is navigating uncertainty, changing symptoms and fragmented information. The site should help them feel understood, calmer and more in control.

Use imagery emotionally

Photography and illustration should reflect the lived experience of the actual customer, not simply generic wellness visuals.

Create shareable moments

If the report reveals a meaningful pattern, make that experience easy to share with a partner, friend or clinician. The product can become a story people naturally pass on.

Overall direction

BetterSkin already has the foundations of a compelling product story. The biggest improvement is to simplify the landing page around a clear belief system, visually demonstrate the connected-body idea, elevate the weekly report, reduce explanatory clutter and add the proof and human story needed to make the promise feel credible.
